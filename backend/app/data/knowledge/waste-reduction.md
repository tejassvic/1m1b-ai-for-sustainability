## Why waste matters

Every product carries an invisible history: the water, energy and raw materials
used to make it, plus the emissions of moving it. Waste is therefore not only a
disposal problem — it is a signal that resources were extracted and never fully
used. Cutting waste at the source usually removes emissions upstream, whereas
recycling recovers only part of the value already spent.

## The practical hierarchy

Most waste guidance follows one order of preference:

1. **Refuse** what you do not need — the single most effective step, because
   nothing has to be produced, transported or processed.
2. **Reduce** what you do need: buy loose produce, choose refillable containers,
   print double-sided, and repair before replacing.
3. **Reuse** — bottles, bags, containers, clothing and electronics can have a
   second and third life, especially through sharing and repair networks.
4. **Recycle** cleanly. Contaminated recycling streams are frequently rejected,
   so rinsing and sorting matters as much as putting the item in the right bin.
5. **Rot** — composting food scraps returns nutrients to soil and avoids the
   methane that food waste generates in landfill.

## On a campus or in a workplace

Segregated bins only work when they are labelled, emptied reliably and placed
where the decision is made — beside the canteen counter, not at the gate.
Reusable crockery, a refill water station and a paperless default for
administrative processes each remove a recurring stream rather than a single
item.

## Food waste specifically

Roughly a third of food produced globally is never eaten. Practical measures
include planning meals around what is already in the kitchen, storing food
correctly, using leftovers deliberately, and composting what cannot be eaten.
Institutions can track plate waste for a few weeks; the pattern is usually
concentrated in a small number of habits.

## How to tell it is working

Count bin lifts, not intentions. A monthly weight or volume check of the general
waste bin — before and after a change — gives a clear, honest measure, and
reveals whether a new habit actually held.
