## What SDG 12 commits to

Sustainable Development Goal 12 seeks to ensure sustainable consumption and
production patterns. Its targets address material and resource efficiency,
halving per-capita global food waste, environmentally sound management of
chemicals and waste, reducing waste generation through prevention, reduction,
recycling and reuse, corporate sustainability reporting, and procurement
practices that favour sustainable options. It also commits to raising awareness
for sustainable lifestyles.

## The core idea: the loop, not the line

Conventional consumption runs in a straight line — extract, make, use, discard.
A sustainable approach closes the loop so materials are kept in use: designed
for durability and repair, recovered at end of life, and fed back as inputs.
Prevention sits at the top of that hierarchy because nothing that is never made
has to be recovered.

## Where the leverage is

For most organisations, the largest share of environmental impact sits upstream
in procurement rather than in daily operations. That means the buying decision —
what is specified, how much, and for how long — is usually the most powerful
lever available. Specifications that demand repairability, take-back or recycled
content change supplier behaviour across a whole market.

## Practical steps

- **Measure what you buy.** A spend-and-quantity baseline by category is enough
  to start.
- **Change specifications.** Require durability, repairability and end-of-life
  recovery in tenders.
- **Repair and reuse internally**, including furniture, devices and equipment.
- **Segregate waste properly** and contract for verified, traceable disposal.
- **Report honestly**, including scope boundaries and what did not improve.

## Consumer-facing side

Choices matter most when they are repeated. Durable purchases, refill and repair
services, second-hand markets and buying less overall all reduce throughput.
Information works best when it is available at the moment of decision — at the
shelf, the checkout or the booking page — rather than in a report published
later.

## A caution

Efficiency gains can be cancelled by increased volume if consumption keeps
growing. Absolute impact — total resource use, not intensity per unit — is the
measure that matters.
