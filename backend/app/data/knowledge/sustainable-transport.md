## Why transport dominates personal footprints

Road transport is one of the largest sources of energy-related emissions
worldwide, and private car use accounts for a substantial share of a typical
household footprint. Two properties make it unusually tractable: emissions scale
almost linearly with distance travelled, and the alternatives — walking,
cycling, public transport — are known and already available in many places.

## The shift that matters most

Moving a regular journey from a private car to public transport, cycling or
walking removes combustion rather than reducing it. Because a commute repeats,
a change made once is applied hundreds of times a year. This is why modal shift
usually outperforms every other transport measure an individual can take.

Where public transport is not yet good enough, the same logic still applies at
the margin: combining trips, avoiding the least efficient single-occupancy
journeys, and using park-and-ride options.

## Vehicle-side measures

For journeys that must be driven, the largest factors are occupancy and vehicle
size. A shared car divides emissions between passengers; a smaller, lighter
vehicle uses less energy regardless of fuel type. Electric vehicles reduce
emissions substantially where the grid is relatively clean, and continue to
improve as the grid does. Efficient driving behaviour — steady speeds, correct
tyre pressure, avoiding unnecessary idling — delivers a modest but immediate
reduction.

## Institutions and campuses

The highest-leverage measures are structural rather than persuasive: secure
cycle parking and showers, subsidised transit passes, shuttle services on
high-demand routes, remote-work options where roles allow, and parking pricing
that reflects the space consumed. Charging infrastructure for electric two- and
four-wheelers follows demand, but provision of safe walking and cycling routes
usually precedes it.

## Measuring it

Record passenger-kilometres and mode share, not just trips. A rising total with a
rising sustainable share tells a very different story from a falling total with
a rising car share.

## Co-benefits

Active travel reduces air pollution and traffic injury risk, and supports
population physical activity — health gains that accrue immediately and locally,
independent of any climate benefit.
