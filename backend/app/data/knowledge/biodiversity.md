## Why biodiversity is infrastructure, not decoration

A diverse ecosystem is more stable under stress. Mixed species plantings resist
pest outbreaks and disease better than monocultures, varied root structures hold
slopes and soil more effectively, and diverse pollinator communities support
food production. Biodiversity is therefore a functional component of resilience,
including climate resilience, rather than a separate concern.

## What actually damages it

The dominant pressures are habitat loss and fragmentation, land-use change,
over-exploitation, pollution, invasive species and climate change. Fragmentation
deserves particular attention: a habitat divided into small isolated patches
loses species even when the total area is unchanged, because populations can no
longer move, breed or recolonise.

## Practical restoration principles

**Use local and native species.** They are adapted to the climate, soil and
season, and they support the insects and birds that depend on them. Native
planting also establishes without irrigation or fertiliser in most cases.

**Connect rather than isolate.** Corridors, hedgerows, riparian strips and
stepping-stone patches restore movement. A modest corridor between two good
patches often achieves more than expanding either one.

**Diversify structure, not just species count.** Varying canopy height, age and
ground cover creates niches. A structurally simple planting supports far fewer
species than its species list suggests.

**Protect what remains first.** Preventing the loss of an intact habitat is
almost always cheaper and more effective than restoring a degraded one.

**Manage invasives.** Early removal is dramatically cheaper than containment
later, and removal should be paired with replanting so the cleared ground is not
promptly recolonised.

## Water and soil

Healthy soils store carbon and water and are the foundation of terrestrial
biodiversity. Contour treatment, mulch, cover crops and reduced tillage all
protect soil structure. Riparian vegetation shades waterways, moderates
temperature and filters runoff.

## Urban settings

Green corridors along drains and rail lines, pollinator-friendly planting,
reduced mowing and permeable surfaces each support urban biodiversity. Small,
connected interventions outperform large isolated ones.

## Monitoring

Repeat the same simple observations — a fixed transect, a set of photo points, a
species list — at the same season each year. Consistency over years is more
informative than intensity in a single survey.
