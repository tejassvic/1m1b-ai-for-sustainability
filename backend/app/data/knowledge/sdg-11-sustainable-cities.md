## What SDG 11 commits to

Sustainable Development Goal 11 aims to make cities and human settlements
inclusive, safe, resilient and sustainable. Its targets cover adequate and
affordable housing, accessible and sustainable transport systems, participatory
and integrated urban planning, protection of cultural and natural heritage,
reduced environmental impact of cities (including air quality and waste
management), and expanded green and public space.

## Why it matters beyond cities

More than half the world's population lives in urban areas, and that share keeps
rising. Because cities concentrate both emissions and exposure — to heat, to
flooding, to air pollution — they are where climate and development agendas
either succeed or stall.

## Resilience as a design requirement

A resilient settlement is one where a shock does not become a catastrophe. That
depends on choices made long before any event: where building is permitted, how
drainage and green space are maintained, how easily people can reach safety,
and whether essential services are redundant rather than a single point of
failure. Nature-based measures — tree canopy, permeable surfaces, restored
waterways — are now standard elements of this design vocabulary.

## Access and equity

Transport and housing determine who can reach work, school and healthcare. When
these are treated purely as markets, the least mobile residents lose the most.
The goal's emphasis on "inclusive" is a reminder that a sustainable city which
excludes people is not a sustainable city.

## What an organisation can do

- Support mixed-use, transit-oriented development rather than car-dependent
  sprawl.
- Protect and expand green space and tree canopy.
- Improve waste segregation and safe disposal at the neighbourhood level.
- Make participation real: publish plans, hold meetings at accessible times and
  places, and respond to submissions.
- Assess climate risk in every siting and infrastructure decision.

## Measuring it

Useful indicators tend to be spatial and distributional: share of the population
within walking distance of frequent transit, area of green space per capita,
air quality by neighbourhood, and the proportion of residents living in
settlements at high hazard exposure.
